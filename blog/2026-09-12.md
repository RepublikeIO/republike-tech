# Saturday, September 12th 2026

## The backlog goes live: Discover, threads, push, and posts with structure

### The release in one sentence

After a quiet few weeks of building, a large release went to production this week, covering the feed, the thread view, the recognition gesture, the post content model, push notifications, onboarding, and the marketing site, so the product people actually touch now matches the direction we have been writing down.

### Discover, and a feed that rations attention

"For you" is now "Discover", and the name is not cosmetic. The old feed leaned on raw popularity. The new one selects from a pool and ranks with an exposure floor, so a contribution gets a fair chance to be seen before the numbers decide anything.

To do that honestly we had to know what was actually served, not just what was read. Every post shown to a member now records a feed impression, one row per post and member. That drives seen demotion (you stop getting the same thing over and over), the distinct-member count behind the exposure floor, and the instrumentation we use to check the feed is doing what we claim. Impressions are exposure, not engagement, and they are kept separate from reactions on purpose.

### Threads that actually nest

The reply screen is recursive now. The same screen works at every depth, so a reply to a reply to a reply is not a special case that renders differently or loses its place. Opening a thread loads the full ancestor chain, so you land with the context above you already in place instead of a fragment.

A featured reply can sit under a post without a header strip announcing it, and moving from the feed into a thread is a soft navigation that keeps the post you tapped painted instead of reloading the page from zero. Small things, but they are the difference between a thread that feels native and one that feels like a set of separate pages stapled together.

### One deliberate gesture, not a scoreboard

The reaction is a single door under a post, carrying the Republike mark, not a heart. It opens a panel that asks what the contribution brought you: an adjective (it made me think, it was useful, it was inspiring), pick one, and an optional stance (agree or disagree) that stays private and is never counted. The three old conduct axes are gone, and the server rejects them: judging whether something breaks the principles is a job for people, not a one-tap label. The stance is recorded for the author and the model, never published as a number, because a running tally of who disagreed is a balance of power, not information.

### Posts have structure now

Posts are moving from stored HTML to a structured document (the editor's own JSON), and on read the app prefers the structured version. This is the groundwork for treating a post as content with parts (text, mentions, links) rather than a blob of markup we have to parse and trust. The write path keeps both for now so nothing old breaks while the structured form becomes the source of truth.

### Public by default

A contribution is public unless a paid member deliberately makes it private. The default visibility is public, replies are always public, and a public thread gets public replies. This closes a long-standing mismatch where the product said "your posts are public" while the write path quietly defaulted the other way, and it means free and signed-out readers see the conversation the way it was meant to be read.

### Push, and knowing when someone replied

Push notifications have real infrastructure now, across both Apple and Google, with per-type preferences so a member controls what actually reaches their phone. On top of that, a member is told when someone comments on their post, which is the notification people most expected and most missed.

### Onboarding and the words at the paywall

The stepped signup flow is built from shared parts rather than a pile of one-off screens, and the wording at the principles, beta and paywall steps was brought in line with the real design instead of a screenshot stretched to fit. Unique names now follow a clear rule set, ASCII only and locked once chosen, so a handle means one thing. The onboarding funnel is measurable end to end without identity columns, so we can see where people drop off without watching individuals.

### The marketing site is one page, and the mark moves

The marketing site was stripped down to a single page that says what Republike is and gets out of the way. The brand mark animates, converging on a logo tap and on pull to refresh, so the symbol is a live thing in the product and not a static asset. A smart link sends people to the right place automatically, the app stores on a phone and the web on a desktop, from one URL.

### On mobile

All of this reached the app as well, which shipped to the stores this week: Discover and the new ranking, the recursive threads, the single recognition gesture, push, and the aligned onboarding. The app and the backend now speak the same language.

### What's next

- **The full recognition model.** The interim gesture is live; the next step is the panel that names what a contribution brought in plain language, a daily budget so recognition is deliberate, and a quiet private thank-you that costs nothing and is seen only by the author.
- **Citizen moderation, end to end.** A durable conduct record, a jury drawn by lot with real sortition and a stuck-case escape hatch, and an appeal a member can actually file. A confirmed decision should carry weight only once it can be examined and contested.
- **Recovering failed renewals.** Native dunning, a clear past-due signal in the product, and the instrumentation to see the renewal funnel instead of finding out about it a quarter late.
- **A structured feed on the read side too**, so the public recognition a contribution has earned can show up where it is read, not only where it is made.
