# Wednesday, September 30th 2026

## Recognition in your own words, reputation you read instead of rank

### The week in one sentence

Recognition grew up: the gesture now answers in the member's own voice, the numeric score and the leaderboard that ranked people by it are gone, and the qualification graph we started writing last week now feeds a reputation you read rather than a number you climb.

### Recognition, in the first person

Last week recognition spoke in the product's voice: an insight, a step forward, a change of mind. This week it speaks in yours. The panel asks what a contribution brought you and gives you four plain answers to pick from: it helped me understand better, it made me see things differently, it made me want to act, it resonated with me. First person on purpose, because the sentence belongs to the reader, not to Republike.

The four carry the same weight and the same cost, and the order they appear in is fixed per member rather than the same for everyone, so the first slot never quietly becomes the default choice that skews the data we later read.

The quiet thank-you that sat beside recognition last week has been removed. It was a second, softer way to appreciate a post, and two gestures for one intention diluted both. Recognition is the gesture now, and it says enough on its own.

The budget that rations it opened up: three qualifications back a day instead of one, up to ten held in reserve instead of seven. Both numbers now live in server configuration rather than baked into the app, so we can tune scarcity as we watch how members actually use it, without shipping a new build to change a number. And when someone reaches for a qualification they do not have, the product records that moment, because the only way to know whether the ceiling is set right is to see how often it binds.

### Reputation you read, not a score you rank by

The number is gone. The public leaderboard that ranked members by a reputation score has been removed, and the score itself no longer fronts the profile. What replaces it is quieter and truer to the model: a profile now shows the contributions a member has been recognised for, not a figure that invites comparison. Reputation here is something you read about a person, not a rank you sort them into.

Under that surface the graph does real work. The qualifications and conduct we started recording are now computed into the pillars that describe a member: the quality their contributions are recognised for, and the respect they earn by how they conduct themselves. Those pillars are derived from what actually happened, the recognitions given and the conduct on record, rather than from a formula tuned to produce a ranking.

The last of the old token vocabulary was also taken out of the surfaces a member sees. It belonged to an earlier idea of the product and only added noise to the one we are building.

### Recognition where it is read, now in the feed

Last week a contribution that earned recognition said so where it was read on its own page. This week the private reminder of what you gave follows you into the feed as well: qualify a post, and the line that tells you what you recognised it for is there on the feed card, not only inside the thread. Recognition you could only see by opening the post was recognition you would rarely see.

### Closing the loop on a failed payment

The renewal-recovery work from last week gained its last, firmest step. A subscription that has genuinely lapsed, once the retries and the reminders have run their course, now cleanly withdraws access instead of leaving an account in a half-paid limbo. Recovery is patient up to the point where it stops being recovery, and then it is honest about it.

### The app caught up

Everything above reached the phone. The recognition panel is now a native sheet that opens in place, rather than a walkthrough bolted onto the old picker, so choosing an answer feels like part of the app instead of a tour of it. The thread became a pure reading surface: opening a post no longer fights you for the keyboard, and replying happens on its own screen with the post you are answering kept in view. Onboarding stopped clipping its own text on taller screens, members whose subscription had lapsed can reply again, and a small footer now shows the version and build number, so a bug report can say exactly which app it came from.

### What's next

- **The public recognition line in the feed.** The line that marks a recognised contribution is live where a post is read on its own; carrying it across the whole feed without a query per post is the next step, which means holding a recognition state on the post rather than recomputing it on every read.
- **Moderation on the phone.** The contest-and-appeal procedure is built on the web; bringing the full flow to the app is the work that follows.
- **Letting the structured post become the source of truth**, and retiring the old markup path once the read side has fully moved across.
