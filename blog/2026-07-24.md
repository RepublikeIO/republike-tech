# Friday, July 24th 2026

## Contracts that mean what they say — including free tier

### The week in one sentence

We spent the week making free-tier behaviour match the access contract we already wrote down: free citizens are not moderators, free posts are public, and paid citizens keep the private surface — then shipped that enforcement on the server so the UI cannot lie about what the API will accept.

### Free citizens are not invited to moderate

The access map has been clear for a while: `FREE_CITIZEN` has no `moderation:vote`. What still happened at signup was a leftover product path that created a first-moderation invite for every new account, including free ones. A free user opened the app, saw an invitation to moderate, and either bounced off a capability wall or looked like the platform was broken.

That path is closed.

- **Signup no longer creates a first-moderation invite.** Free activation grants a free contract and stops there.
- **The invite is created when a paid plan actually grants the contract** that includes moderation — not before.
- **Notification reads are gated.** If a leftover `MODERATION` invite still exists for someone without the capability, it is hidden and does not inflate the unread badge.
- **Activating free tier revokes an ineligible leftover invite** so older accounts clean themselves up on the free path.

Same posture as the June 26 contract work: the database and the capability map enforce the rule; we do not rely on “remember not to invite free users.”

### Private posts are a paid capability

The other free-tier leak was visibility. Paid citizens can publish private posts and read others’ private posts (`posts:view:private`). Free citizens cannot. That was true on the *read* path for a long time — the feed and thread queries already filtered private content for free and anonymous viewers. The *write* path and several clients still let free authors set PRIVATE, and the default when visibility was omitted was PRIVATE, which is the opposite of “free posts are public.”

This week we closed that end to end.

**Server (source of truth).** Create and edit resolve the author’s role and force `PUBLIC` when they lack the paid private capability. Omitted visibility defaults to `PUBLIC`, not `PRIVATE`. Free authors cannot flip a post private through the API.

**Web composer.** Free users see a locked public label — no private/public dropdown. Free polls no longer force private (they stay public). Paid polls still default private as before.

**Post page SSR.** `/p/[id]` used to let any logged-in session open a private post that was not theirs. It now uses the same gate as the feed: author, or paid viewer with `posts:view:private`. Private posts do not get rich open-graph meta for crawlers.

**Search and fan-out.** Public posts only go into Algolia; search re-applies the visibility filter after hits. “New post” emails only fire for public posts. Mentions on private posts only notify people who can actually open them.

**Mobile.** The poll/survey composer already coerced free users to public. The remaining content-creation screens now do the same: wire payload is public without the capability, and the privacy control is a no-op for free tier.

All of that is on **dev** and **staging** for the webapp. The free-tier composer alignment on mobile is on a PR into `develop`.

### Onboarding kept getting real

Alongside the contract work, onboarding continued to harden rather than grow new one-off screens.

The stepped register flow was factored into shared parts — step shell, primary action, resend countdown, brand tokens, a session hook for “who is signing up” — so the funnel we started measuring last Friday is made of pieces we can change once. Dead legacy signup screens that nothing routes to were removed.

Phone verification by SMS sits next to the email code path. The country picker stopped drawing flags as emoji (empty boxes on Android) and uses bundled SVG art instead. Verification errors that used to collapse into a generic “start failed” are being treated as a real diagnostic problem, not a one-line toast — when every layer says `DELIVERED` and the handset is silent, that is carrier filtering, not a broken send pipeline, and the client needs a reason that says so.

Web onboarding principles and beta screens were brought into line with the desktop Figma frames (not a mobile screenshot stretched to a monitor). Product analytics for onboarding drop-off views and a query pack for the warehouse role landed so the funnel is queryable without identity columns.

### Android 16, and the target that lied

Play’s API-level deadline forced the bump to **Android 16 (API 36)**. The first attempt did nothing: the app module hardcoded an older `targetSdk` and overrode the shared project value. Deleting the local override and verifying the *shipped* manifest is what actually cleared the bar. The billing-library major bump is deliberately not in this window — it touches IAP end to end and gets its own test pass.

### Reactions: one heart, no public scoreboard

Mobile feed reactions moved to a single LIKE heart, and public like / reply / repost / tip counters are hidden on the card footer. The product direction is Threads-shaped: reaction without turning every post into a scoreboard. That shipped on mobile `develop` this week.
